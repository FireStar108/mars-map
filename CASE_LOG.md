### Mission terrain

- Source: HiRISE stereo DTM `DTEEC_019698_1750_019988_1750_U01`, PDS dataset `MRO-M-HIRISE-5-DTM-V1.0`, produced by the University of Arizona HiRISE team. The local PDS/ortho label identifies the source products and map projection.
- Archive and license: [NASA/USGS HiRISE DTMs](https://registry.opendata.aws/nasa-usgs-mars-hirise-dtms/), CC0 1.0, [DOI 10.5066/P9VQTS7V](https://doi.org/10.5066/P9VQTS7V).
- The three playable areas—Boxwork Ridges, Gediz Vallis Channel, and Uncharted Sulfate Slopes—are in Gale crater and use coordinates recorded in `js/zones.js`.
- `data/scripts/build_zones.py` samples local terrain around each zone and creates compact near/far height grids, sky-view layers, and map textures under `assets/zones/`.
- The mission terrain textures are DTM-derived shaded relief; the locally available HiRISE JP2 orthoimage is not currently used as the color texture. The sand layer is zero-filled because a validated sand mask has not been produced. These are implementation limitations, not observed surface claims.
- Far-terrain DTM no-data samples are filled from nearby valid samples so the horizon mesh can be rendered; those filled edge elevations are not measurements.

### Model assumptions

- Two 8-neighbor A* searches use shortest 3D distance or distance × [exp(3.5 × |grade|) + 5 × (terrain slope/28)²]. Invalid cells cannot be crossed diagonally. Routes snap base and destination to 5 m grid cell centers. Both legs use the same corridor.
- Suit baseline speed 0.65 m/s. Speed factors exp(−3.5 × |grade|), 1/(1+slope/90), and 1/(1+1.2 × positive grade). Return time reverses the signed grade. Weather factor 1/[1 + .012×wind + .007×max(0,−40−T) + max(0,500−visibility)/500]. These are educational design coefficients, not calibrated astronaut physiology.
- Risk index sums terrain (maximum slope/28 ×23 plus steep-distance proportion ×20), wind up to15, cold up to12, visibility up to20, and reserve shortfall up to30; clipped at100. It is not an accident probability. The lowest risk feasible route is recommended, with total time breaking ties.
- Feasibility requires outbound+science+return+reserve ≤ budget. Scenario holds: wind≥25 m/s, visibility<100 m, temperature<−100°C. These are prototype limits, not NASA safety standards.
- Temperature, wind and visibility are constant/uniform scenarios. Random weather is independent sampling within control ranges, not a climatological distribution. MEDA record is historical, not a forecast. Wind arrows are illustrative and do not affect route bearings. No radiation or suit consumable model is claimed for this planner.

### Evidence interpretation and model limits

- ODSC sample counts by Mars year are MY26:6, MY27:189, MY28:215, MY29:37, MY30:1 (roughly2004–2010). These are historical detections with highly uneven observation coverage and repeat observations of some storms. They cannot establish regional annual frequency, probability or present weather without an observation-coverage denominator.
- Seasonal heat field counts event centroids within5° of each grid cell, in a90° Ls window. Heat halos are counting neighborhoods, not measured storm footprints. Routing normalizes by the largest count in that season; indices across seasons are not absolute climatological comparisons. Low counts explicitly retain sparse-coverage uncertainty.
- Radiation layer uses exp(elevation/10800m), a dimensionless inverse-atmospheric-column proxy normalized to datum altitude. This is a design assumption for comparative routing. It is not calibrated dose, a transport calculation, a map of radioactive soil or a solar-event forecast. Secondary particles, regolith, material shielding and solar modulation are unresolved.
- Weighted route edge cost is length × [1 + dustWeight × normalized detection count + radiationWeight × altitude proxy]. Both candidate routes receive the same integrated score. An avoidance path may reduce one index while increasing distance enough that the shortest path is still recommended. No claim of universally safe or globally optimal operational routing is made.
